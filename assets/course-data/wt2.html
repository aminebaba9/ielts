💬 Opinion
🏫 IELTS Academic Writing Task 2

Some people think that the only way to ensure the survival of wild animals is to keep them in zoos and wildlife parks. Others believe that it is wrong to keep animals in captivity, and that more effort should be made to protect their natural habitats.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Write at least 250 words. You should spend about 40 minutes on this task.

Prompt Analysis — What is being asked?

🎯
Task Type Identification This prompt says "discuss both views AND give your own opinion" — this is technically a hybrid (discussion + opinion). You must briefly address both sides, but your opinion must be clear and dominant. This is one of the most common Opinion-type framings.
⚠️
Common Mistake to Avoid Many students give equal weight to both sides and never state a clear position. At Band 8–9, your opinion must be stated in the introduction and reinforced in the conclusion. Don't sit on the fence with no real stance.
✅
Winning Strategy State a clear position (e.g. "I believe habitat protection is ultimately more important"). Devote one body paragraph to the opposing view (zoos), and one full paragraph to your supported view. Conclude by reinforcing your opinion.

⭐ Band 9
📝 Word count: ~290 ⏱ Type: Opinion (discuss both views) 🎯 Topic: Environment / Animal Rights

The question of how best to protect endangered wildlife has become increasingly urgent in an age of mass habitat destruction and climate change. While some argue that captive environments such as zoos offer an effective safeguard for at-risk species, I am firmly of the view that preserving natural ecosystems is a far more sustainable and ethical solution.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Context-Setting Opening
Rather than paraphrasing the prompt mechanically, this sentence places the issue in a broader real-world context ("mass habitat destruction and climate change"). This signals sophisticated engagement with the topic — a Band 8–9 marker.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Thesis Statement — Clear Position
"I am firmly of the view that..." immediately establishes an unambiguous personal stance. The contrast ("While some argue... I believe...") acknowledges both views without sitting on the fence. This earns maximum Task Achievement marks.

Proponents of zoos contend that captive breeding programmes have demonstrably rescued species from extinction. The Arabian oryx and the California condor are frequently cited as examples of animals that survived only through human intervention. Furthermore, modern zoos generate public awareness and raise funds that can be redirected towards conservation efforts in the wild. These are not insignificant contributions.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Conceding the Opposing View Fairly
A Band 9 writer does not dismiss the opposing view — they engage with it seriously before countering it. "Demonstrably rescued," "are not insignificant contributions" — these phrases show intellectual honesty, boosting coherence and Task Achievement scores.

Nevertheless, I would argue that the wholesale preservation of wild habitats addresses the root cause of wildlife decline in a way that captivity fundamentally cannot. Animals confined to artificial environments are typically unable to develop the behaviours essential for survival in the wild, effectively removing them from the ecological systems they evolved to inhabit. More critically, the scale of global biodiversity loss demands a systemic response: protecting and restoring the forests, wetlands, and savannahs that sustain entire ecosystems, not merely isolated species.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Contrast Linker + Position Reinforcement
"Nevertheless, I would argue that..." is a sophisticated contrast marker. The phrase "addresses the root cause" introduces analytical thinking — not just stating a view, but explaining WHY it is superior. This is exactly what separates Band 7 from Band 9.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Advanced Lexical Resource
"Ecological systems," "evolved to inhabit," "confinement" — topic-specific vocabulary used naturally and accurately. Notice the complex noun phrase "behaviours essential for survival in the wild" — this grammatical complexity (post-modification) is characteristic of Band 8–9 writing.

In conclusion, although captive conservation has its merits, I maintain that directing greater resources towards the protection and rehabilitation of natural habitats offers the only truly viable path to long-term species survival. Zoos may buy time for individual species, but it is only in their natural environments that animals — and the wider ecosystems they support — can truly thrive.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Conclusion — Concede + Reinforce Structure
"Although... I maintain that..." is the ideal conclusion structure for this essay type: briefly acknowledging the opposing view while firmly restating your own position. Never introduce new ideas in the conclusion. The final sentence ("buy time... truly thrive") ends with a memorable, image-rich statement.

💬 Opinion

The 4-Paragraph Opinion Essay Formula

For "discuss both views + give your opinion" prompts, this structure consistently achieves top marks:

Introduction (~50 words)

  • Context sentence (NOT a copy of the prompt)
  • Acknowledge opposing view briefly
  • Clear thesis: state YOUR position explicitly
  • Use: "I firmly believe / I am convinced that..."

Body ¶1 — The Other View (~80 words)

  • Present the opposing view fairly
  • Give 1–2 supporting examples or reasons
  • Acknowledge its merit without agreeing
  • End with a subtle pivot toward your view

Body ¶2 — Your View (~90 words)

  • Open with a strong contrast linker
  • State your position clearly and directly
  • Provide 2 reasons + 1 example/evidence
  • Explain WHY your view is superior

Conclusion (~40 words)

  • No new ideas — only synthesis
  • Briefly concede the other view
  • Restate YOUR position firmly
  • End with a forward-looking or impactful statement
Word balance:
Intro · Body 1 · Body 2 · Conclusion

Sentence-Level Patterns for Each Paragraph

📌
Introduction Formula [Context] + [Concede opposing view with "While/Although..."] + [State YOUR view with "I firmly believe / I am of the view that..."]
📌
Body ¶1 Formula [Topic sentence presenting THEIR view] + [Evidence/example] + [Pivot: "These are not insignificant points, however..."]
📌
Body ¶2 Formula [Nevertheless / However + YOUR view] + [Reason 1 + explanation] + [Reason 2 + example] + [Closing impact sentence]
📌
Conclusion Formula "In conclusion, although [opposing view], I maintain that [YOUR view]." + [Forward-looking final sentence]

⏱ Planning

Why 80% of Band 6 Essays Fail in the First 5 Minutes

Most low-band essays show no evidence of planning. Band 8–9 candidates invest 5 minutes to build a skeleton that guarantees coherence.

🧭
The 5-Minute Drill for Opinion / Agree or Disagree Minute 1: Dissect prompt — underline instruction words. Minute 2: Decide position — thesis in 12 words. Minute 3: Generate 2 core ideas + 1 example each. Minute 4: Allocate to paragraphs. Minute 5: Select 3 high-level linkers and 2 collocations.

Minute 1-2: Position Lock

  • Ask: Single or double question?
  • Choose 70/30 or 100% — never vague
  • Write thesis: "I firmly believe X because A and B"
  • This sentence guides entire essay

Minute 3: Idea Generation

  • Use WHY-HOW-EFFECT chain
  • Why? How manifest? What effect?
  • Attach concrete example
  • Reject generic "bad for society"

Minute 4: Blueprint

  • Para1: Opposing view + concession
  • Para2: Strongest argument + evidence
  • Ensure one clear topic per para
  • Check logical flow

Minute 5: Language Priming

  • Pre-select 2 complex sentences
  • Pre-select 3 academic collocations
  • Pre-select contrast + cause linkers
  • Prevents mid-essay panic
⚡ 40-30-20-10 Rule: 40 min = 5 planning, 30 writing (7 intro, 10 body1, 10 body2, 3 conclusion), 5 checking. Set silent timer.

📚 Band 9

The 4 Complex Sentence Types Examiners Count

At Band 8–9 you need deliberate complexity, not random.

1
Conditional (If/Unless/Provided that) "Provided that governments invest in corridors, species can recover without captivity." Shows hypothetical reasoning.
2
Relative with Preposition "Ecosystems on which species depend are disappearing." Using "on which, in which" signals Band 9.
3
Concessive (Although/While) "Although zoos offer protection, they fail to address habitat loss." Essential for balanced thinking.
4
Cleft for Emphasis "It is habitat destruction, not lack of breeding, that drives extinction." High impact for intro/conclusion.
⚡ One of Each per Essay: Aim for 1 conditional, 1 relative with preposition, 1 concessive, 1 cleft. Highlight in plan.

✏️ Practice Prompt 1

In many countries, the government is spending money on improving the railway infrastructure. Some people think that improving roads is more beneficial. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Write at least 250 words. Time yourself: 40 minutes.

⭐ Band 9 Model Answer — Practice Prompt 1

📝 Word count: ~250⏱ Type: Opinion (agree/disagree)🎯 Topic: Transport / Infrastructure

Governments across the world face difficult choices when allocating limited transport budgets, and one of the most contentious debates concerns whether funds are better directed towards railways or roads. While road improvements undoubtedly offer certain short-term benefits, I firmly disagree with the view that they are more beneficial than investment in rail infrastructure.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Framing as a Genuine Policy Dilemma
Presenting this as a real "difficult choice" governments face, rather than a simple binary, signals mature engagement with the topic from the very first sentence.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Clear, Unambiguous Thesis
"I firmly disagree" leaves no doubt about the writer's position — essential for a Band 8–9 Opinion essay, where the stance must be clear from the introduction onward.

Proponents of road investment argue that most people rely on cars and buses for daily transport, particularly in areas poorly served by rail networks. Improved roads can reduce journey times, ease local congestion, and support businesses that depend on road freight for the delivery of goods. In rural regions especially, where laying new railway lines would be prohibitively expensive, upgrading existing roads may indeed represent the more practical and cost-effective solution.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Fair, Developed Concession
The opposing view is given real substance (rural access, cost) rather than being dismissed quickly — this fairness is what earns full Task Achievement marks for the concession paragraph.

Nevertheless, I would argue that railway investment delivers far greater long-term benefits, particularly in terms of environmental sustainability and capacity. Trains produce substantially lower carbon emissions per passenger than cars, making rail expansion a crucial component of any serious strategy to combat climate change. Furthermore, a single railway line can transport vastly more passengers than an equivalent road, meaning that rail investment addresses congestion more efficiently than simply building additional lanes, which tend to fill with new traffic almost as soon as they open — a well-documented phenomenon known as induced demand.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Two Named Criteria for Comparison
"Environmental sustainability and capacity" gives the reader a clear analytical framework for the paragraph that follows — a hallmark of well-organised Band 9 argumentation.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Named Technical Concept
"Induced demand" is a precise, real technical term from transport economics — using it correctly and explaining it briefly in context is a strong Lexical Resource signal.

In conclusion, although road improvements offer practical benefits in certain contexts, I remain convinced that prioritising railway infrastructure represents the wiser long-term investment, given its superior environmental and capacity advantages.

⭐ Band 9 Model Answer — Practice Prompt 2

📝 Word count: ~265⏱ Type: Discuss Both + Opinion🎯 Topic: Education / Finance

The question of who should bear the financial burden of university education — the state or the individual — remains a deeply divisive issue worldwide. While there are compelling arguments on both sides, I lean towards the view that some form of student contribution is ultimately fairer and more sustainable than entirely free tuition.

✦ Band 9 Technique
"I lean towards" — Measured Discussion Register
Softer than "I firmly believe," this phrase is correctly calibrated for a discuss-both-views-plus-opinion prompt, where genuine weighing of both sides is expected before a measured conclusion.

Advocates of free university education argue that it removes financial barriers, ensuring that talented students from low-income backgrounds are not excluded from higher education simply because they cannot afford it. Countries such as Germany and Norway, which offer tuition-free university education, often point to higher rates of social mobility as evidence of this policy's success. Free education can also be seen as a long-term investment, since a more highly educated population tends to contribute more in taxes over their working lives.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Named Countries as Evidence
Citing Germany and Norway by name transforms an abstract claim into a verifiable, real-world example — a reliable way to strengthen Task Achievement without overclaiming.

On the other hand, those who support tuition fees contend that a completely free system places an unsustainable burden on taxpayers, many of whom never attend university themselves. Charging fees, particularly when combined with income-contingent loan repayment schemes, ensures that the cost of education is shared more equitably between direct beneficiaries and the wider public. Moreover, when students invest their own money in their education, they may be more motivated to complete their studies and select courses with genuine career value, rather than treating university as a "free" default option.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Taxpayer-Fairness Argument
"Many of whom never attend university themselves" is a specific, logical point about fairness — more persuasive than a vague claim that fees are simply "necessary."
✦ Band 9 Technique
Psychological/Behavioural Reasoning
Linking financial investment to motivation shows analytical depth — connecting an economic fact (paying fees) to a behavioural outcome (completion, course selection).

On balance, while I recognise the appeal of free education in promoting equal access, I believe a well-designed fee system — one that includes generous financial support for disadvantaged students — offers a more balanced and financially sustainable approach for most countries.

✏️ Practice Prompt 3 — recently-reported real exam question

Many people think that the best way to learn a culture is to learn its language. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Write at least 250 words. Time yourself: 40 minutes.

⭐ Band 9 Model Answer — Practice Prompt 3

📝 Word count: ~275 ⏱ Type: Opinion (agree/disagree) 🎯 Topic: Language / Culture

It is frequently claimed that mastering a language provides the most direct route into understanding a culture, since words are inseparable from the values and worldview of the people who speak them. While other means of cultural engagement undoubtedly have merit, I am strongly persuaded that language acquisition remains the single most effective and revealing method of learning about a culture.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Context-Setting Opening
"Words are inseparable from the values and worldview" reframes the prompt analytically rather than repeating it — showing genuine engagement with the underlying idea, not just the surface topic.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Clear Thesis with Fair Acknowledgement
"While other means... undoubtedly have merit, I am strongly persuaded that..." — concedes nuance while still stating an unambiguous position. This balance is exactly what Task Achievement rewards at Band 8–9.

Admittedly, culture can be experienced through numerous channels that do not require linguistic fluency. Travelling to a country, sampling its cuisine, or engaging with its films and literature in translation can all foster a genuine appreciation of local customs and history. Many exchange students, for instance, report profound cultural insights gained simply through daily observation and social immersion, even before they have mastered the host language. These experiences are not insignificant, and they demonstrate that language is not the only doorway into a culture.

✦ Band 9 Technique
"Admittedly" — Genuine Concession
Opening Body ¶1 with "Admittedly" signals honest engagement with the counter-view rather than a token gesture before dismissing it — this is what separates Band 9 concession from a weaker, perfunctory one.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Specific, Realistic Example
"Exchange students... daily observation and social immersion" is concrete and plausible — far stronger than a vague claim like "some people learn culture without language."

Nevertheless, I would argue that language offers a depth of cultural understanding that these other methods cannot fully replicate, since a language encodes centuries of collective experience within its idioms, proverbs, and grammatical structures — concepts that are frequently untranslatable and therefore invisible to those who rely solely on translation or observation. The Japanese concept of "mono no aware", for example, captures a particular sensitivity to the transience of life that has no direct English equivalent, and grasping its nuance requires engagement with the language itself. Moreover, speaking a language fluently allows for genuine, unmediated conversations with native speakers, granting access to humour, sarcasm, and social subtleties that are typically lost through an interpreter or subtitle.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Contrast Linker + Explained Reasoning
"Nevertheless, I would argue... since..." doesn't just restate the position — it explains the underlying mechanism (untranslatable encoded meaning). Explaining WHY is what elevates analysis above simple assertion.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Precise, Culturally-Specific Example
Naming "mono no aware" directly — an untranslatable Japanese concept — is a sophisticated, memorable illustration exactly on-topic. This kind of specific cultural knowledge is a strong Lexical Resource and Task Achievement signal.

In conclusion, although cultural immersion can be achieved through various non-linguistic means, I maintain that language learning offers an unparalleled and irreplaceable depth of cultural insight. Ultimately, to truly inhabit another culture's way of seeing the world, one must first learn to speak in its own words.

📝 Word count: ~275 ⏱ Type: Pure Advantages & Disadvantages 🎯 Topic: Technology / Communication

The rapid advancement of digital communication tools has fundamentally transformed the way people interact, both in professional settings and in their personal lives. This essay will examine the key benefits and drawbacks of technology-mediated communication in place of direct, in-person contact.

✦ Band 8 Technique
Contextual Paraphrase
"Rapid advancement" and "fundamentally transformed" are precise, formal synonyms for the prompt's simpler phrasing. This shows Lexical Resource without direct copying.
✦ Band 8 Technique
Clear Essay Map (Pure Adv/Dis)
"This essay will examine the key benefits and drawbacks" — signals a balanced, objective analysis. No opinion is stated here, which is correct for a pure Adv/Dis prompt.

The most significant advantage of digital communication is its unparalleled capacity to connect individuals across vast geographical distances instantly and at minimal cost. A business executive in London can conduct a video conference with colleagues in Tokyo with no logistical burden whatsoever — a development that has dramatically increased global commercial efficiency. Additionally, technology enables the documentation and storage of communications, allowing messages to be reviewed, retrieved, and shared with precision and ease.

✦ Band 8 Technique
Strong Topic Sentence + Specific Example
"Most significant advantage" clearly signals the paragraph's purpose. The London-Tokyo example is concrete and globally relevant — far stronger than vague statements like "people can communicate everywhere."

On the other hand, reliance on technology for communication carries notable social and professional risks. Face-to-face interaction enables the transmission of non-verbal cues — facial expressions, body language, and tone — which are often entirely absent in digital exchanges. This limitation can lead to significant misunderstandings, particularly in emotionally sensitive or complex negotiations. Moreover, the pervasive use of digital communication has been linked to increased social isolation, as individuals substitute meaningful physical interaction with superficial online exchanges.

✦ Band 8 Technique
Clear Transition to Disadvantages
"On the other hand" cleanly signals the shift to disadvantages. Notice the paragraph doesn't use "However, there are many disadvantages" — it goes straight into the content, which is more sophisticated.
✦ Band 8 Technique
Formal Vocabulary + Hedging
"Has been linked to" is a perfect example of academic hedging — you make the claim without overstating certainty. "Pervasive" and "superficial" demonstrate wide Lexical Resource.

In conclusion, while technology has revolutionised communication by making it faster, more accessible, and more efficient, these gains come at the cost of the depth and nuance that face-to-face interaction uniquely provides. A balanced approach — leveraging digital tools while preserving opportunities for in-person engagement — would appear to represent the most effective path forward.

Introduction (~50 words)

  • State the seriousness/context of the problem
  • Signal that causes AND solutions will be examined

  • Restate the proposed solutions
  • End with a note on feasibility/impact

The root cause of rising congestion is the inadequacy of public transport in many cities, which leaves residents with little viable alternative to private car use. When bus and rail networks are infrequent, overcrowded, or poorly connected, commuters naturally default to driving, regardless of the resulting gridlock. Compounding this issue is the rapid expansion of urban populations, meaning that road infrastructure, largely unchanged for decades, must now accommodate a far greater volume of vehicles than it was originally designed for.

✦ Band 9 Technique
"Root Cause" Analytical Framing
Identifying the ROOT cause (not just a symptom) shows analytical depth. "Leaves residents with little viable alternative" explains the mechanism — WHY inadequate transport leads to more driving — rather than simply stating the fact.
✦ Band 9 Technique
"Compounding This Issue" — Linking Two Causes
This phrase shows how the second cause (population growth) interacts with and worsens the first (inadequate transport) — demonstrating the ability to connect ideas rather than listing them as isolated points.

To address these problems effectively, city authorities must prioritise substantial investment in affordable, reliable public transport, including expanded bus rapid transit systems and metro networks, as has been successfully implemented in cities such as Curitiba and Singapore. Equally important is the introduction of congestion charging schemes, whereby drivers are charged a fee for entering high-traffic zones during peak hours; London's Congestion Charge, for example, has measurably reduced central city traffic while generating revenue that can be reinvested in public transport infrastructure.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Named, Verifiable Real-World Examples
Citing Curitiba and Singapore — cities genuinely known for transport innovation — transforms a generic solution ("invest in public transport") into an evidence-backed argument. This is what separates Band 6–7 solutions from Band 8–9 ones.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Embedded Definition Clause + Specific Result
"Whereby drivers are charged a fee..." is a precise defining relative clause. "Measurably reduced... while generating revenue" gives a concrete, twofold result — showing the solution actually works, not just that it exists.

In conclusion, traffic congestion and air pollution largely stem from insufficient public transport provision and unchecked urban growth. By investing in public transport networks and adopting demand-management measures such as congestion pricing, city authorities can realistically curb these problems and improve urban quality of life.

✅ Self-Assessment Checklist

  • I addressed BOTH causes AND solutions with equal development.
  • My solutions directly address the causes I identified in ¶1.
  • I included at least one specific named example (city, policy, programme).
  • I explained WHY each solution would be effective.
  • Solutions are specific and actionable — not vague ("the government should do more").

📝 Word count: ~265⏱ Type: Discuss Both + Opinion🎯 Topic: Crime / Social Policy

The most effective strategy for reducing crime rates remains a contentious issue, with some advocating for tougher legal penalties while others emphasise addressing the underlying social conditions that contribute to criminal behaviour. Having considered both perspectives, I am inclined to believe that improving social conditions offers a more sustainable long-term solution, although stricter laws retain an important role.

"Having Considered Both..." + Retained Nuance
The thesis leans clearly toward one view while explicitly retaining value in the other ("although stricter laws retain an important role") — avoiding the false extreme of dismissing either side entirely.

Those in favour of stricter laws argue that harsher penalties act as a powerful deterrent, discouraging potential offenders from engaging in criminal activity for fear of severe consequences. Deterrence Mechanism Explained
"Discouraging potential offenders... for fear of severe consequences" explains HOW stricter laws are meant to work, not just that they do — showing genuine analytical engagement with View 1.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Distinguishing Justice from Deterrence
This sentence recognises that punishment can be justified on grounds of justice alone, separate from whether it deters future crime — a genuinely sophisticated distinction few candidates make.

Those on the other side of the debate contend that crime is frequently rooted in poverty, unemployment, and inadequate access to education, meaning that punitive measures alone fail to address its root causes. Individuals lacking stable employment prospects or educational qualifications may turn to crime out of genuine economic necessity, and harsher laws do little to change these underlying circumstances. Countries that have invested heavily in education and job creation programmes, for example, have often seen more sustained reductions in crime than those relying primarily on tougher sentencing.

✦ Band 9 Technique
Root-Cause Framing for View 2
"Fail to address its root causes" mirrors the analytical depth given to View 1 — both sides receive equally rigorous treatment, which is essential for a genuinely balanced Discussion essay.
✦ Band 9 Technique
Comparative Evidence Claim
Rather than a vague appeal, this sentence makes a specific comparative claim (more sustained reductions than punitive approaches) — precisely the kind of evidence-based reasoning examiners reward.

On balance, while stricter laws remain necessary for maintaining order and addressing serious offences, I believe that tackling the social and economic conditions that drive people towards crime offers the more effective long-term solution, since it addresses causes rather than merely consequences.
