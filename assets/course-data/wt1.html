    Introduction The chart illustrates changes in the percentage of households with internet access in Norway, Brazil, and Nigeria over a twenty-year period from 2000 to 2020.
    Overview Overall, internet access rose substantially in all three countries throughout the period, although Norway consistently maintained the highest level of connectivity while Nigeria lagged considerably behind the other two nations at every stage.
    ✦ Overview Technique
    No specific numbers in the Overview
    Notice this paragraph names the two dominant patterns (universal rise + persistent hierarchy Norway>Brazil>Nigeria) WITHOUT quoting any percentages. This is the single most important Band 8–9 discriminator for Task 1 — the Overview is analytical, not numerical.
    Body Paragraph 1 Norway began the period with the highest access rate of the three countries, at just 15%, but this figure climbed steeply to reach 97% by 2020 — meaning that internet access had become almost universal among Norwegian households by the end of the period. Brazil followed a broadly similar upward trajectory, albeit from a much lower starting point of just 5%, eventually reaching 74% by the end of the period.
    ✦ Precision Technique
    Start and end points anchor the description
    "Began... at just 15%... climbed steeply to reach 97%" gives the examiner both boundary values plus a trend verb ("climbed steeply") describing the shape of change between them. This is far more efficient than describing every intermediate year.
    ✦ Comparison Technique
    "Albeit from a much lower starting point"
    This phrase draws an explicit comparison to Norway's trajectory while avoiding needless repetition of "also rose." Comparing across lines like this — rather than describing each in total isolation — is a Band 8–9 coherence marker.
    Body Paragraph 2 By contrast, Nigeria's growth in internet access, while still positive, was far more modest: the figure rose from a mere 1% in 2000 to just 30% by 2020, meaning that more than two-thirds of Nigerian households remained without internet access even at the end of the period.
    ✦ Interpretive Calculation
    "More than two-thirds... remained without"
    This is a calculated inference (100%−30%=70%) rather than a number lifted directly from the chart. Band 8–9 responses occasionally perform this kind of simple derived calculation to add analytical depth — but only when it's accurate and adds insight, not as a gimmick.

    Structure Breakdown
    📈 Line Graph

    The 4-Paragraph Line Graph Formula

    Introduction (~25 words)

    • Paraphrase the chart title
    • State what is measured, for whom, and over what period
    • Never copy the prompt's exact wording

    Overview (~35 words)

    • 2 dominant patterns — NO specific numbers
    • Which line(s) rose/fell overall?
    • Which ended highest/lowest, or crossed another line?

    Body ¶1 (~55 words)

    • Group 2 lines with similar behaviour together
    • Give start/end figures + trend verb
    • Make an explicit comparison between them

    Body ¶2 (~45 words)

    • Describe the remaining line(s)
    • Highlight contrast with Body ¶1's pattern
    • Optional: one derived calculation for insight

    The Grouping Principle

    🔑
    Group by similarity of trend, not by the order given in the legend — Norway and Brazil both show strong, steady growth ending at high levels — they belong together in Body ¶1. Nigeria's much flatter trajectory is different enough to warrant its own paragraph. This grouping logic is what separates a Band 9 response from a mechanical line-by-line description.
    ⏱ Timing: Spend 3–4 minutes planning (identify overview + groupings) before writing. This investment prevents the single biggest time-waster: restructuring paragraphs midway through writing.

    Data Selection Strategy — What to Include, What to Ignore
    Band 9 writers select 5-6 key features — not 15 details. Here's how for Line Graph
    🎯 Selection

    The 80/20 Rule for Line Graph

    In Task 1 you have 20 minutes. You cannot describe everything. Examiners reward overview + key features, not data dumping. For Line Graph, focus on trends, peaks, fluctuations, overall trajectory.

    📊
    Selection Formula for Line Graph
    Step 1: Identify overall trend/pattern in 10 seconds (up? down? stable? transformation?).
    Step 2: Pick 2-3 most significant data points (highest, lowest, most dramatic change).
    Step 3: Group similar data (e.g., "All European countries declined except...").
    Step 4: Ignore minor fluctuations under 5% unless they show a pattern.
    Step 5: Always include a comparison (not just "X was 50%" but "X was double Y").

    ✅ Include

    • Overall trend/change
    • Highest/lowest values with figures
    • Most dramatic increase/decrease
    • Comparisons (twice as much, similar to)
    • Exceptions to the trend

    ❌ Ignore

    • Every single data point
    • Minor 1-2% fluctuations
    • Repeating same structure 5 times
    • Personal opinion ("I think this is good")
    • Data you don't understand — skip smartly
    ⚡ Line Graph Specific Trick: For Line Graph, use the "3+1" method: 3 main features + 1 exception/interesting detail. Example: "While overall sales rose, the most striking feature was the 300% surge in 2020, far exceeding all other years." This shows analytical skill, not just reporting.

    👁 Overview

    Why 60% Fail Task Achievement on Line Graph

    The overview is NOT an introduction. It's a summary of main trends/features, placed after intro, with NO figures. Many students write "The chart shows..." and then jump to details — that's not an overview.

    🏗
    The Universal Overview Formula for Line Graph
    Sentence 1: Overall trend/pattern (no numbers). "Overall, [X] experienced a significant [increase/decline/transformation] over the period, while [Y] remained relatively stable."
    Sentence 2: Most notable detail/comparison (still no numbers, or only general). "In particular, [category] accounted for the largest share throughout, whereas [other] saw the most dramatic change."
    For Maps: "Overall, the area underwent extensive development, transforming from a predominantly rural to a largely urban landscape, with the most notable changes occurring in the north-east."
    For Process: "Overall, the process comprises [number] main stages, beginning with [first] and culminating in [last], and is largely [linear/cyclical/mechanical]."

    Band 6 Overview ❌

    • "There are 5 lines in the graph." (just describes chart)
    • Includes figures: "Sales were 50% in 2000" (that's detail, not overview)
    • Only 1 sentence, vague
    • Placed at end as conclusion

    Band 9 Overview ✅

    • 2 sentences, no figures
    • Uses trend language: surge, plummet, fluctuate, transform
    • Shows comparison: while, whereas, in contrast
    • Placed immediately after introduction
    ⚡ Template Bank for Line Graph: "Overall, it is clear that [general trend]. [Most striking feature] was [category], which [dominant characteristic], while [other categories] showed [secondary pattern]." Memorize 2 templates, adapt with synonyms.

    Comparison & Accuracy Strategy — Language That Scores
    Avoid data inaccuracy — the silent Band 6 killer in Line Graph
    📈 Language

    The 3 Comparison Techniques for Line Graph

    1
    Superlative + Data: "Coal accounted for the largest proportion, at 42% in 2000, more than double that of gas." Not just "Coal was biggest." Always add figure + comparison.
    2
    Relative Change: "The figure for cars rose threefold, from 10% to 30%, whereas bicycles saw only a marginal 2% increase." Use: double, triple, threefold, halved, plummeted by X%.
    3
    Grouping for Cohesion: "Both oil and gas followed a similar upward trajectory, peaking in 2010, in contrast to coal and nuclear, which declined steadily." Grouping = higher Coherence score.
    ⚡ Accuracy Checklist for Line Graph: Before you write a figure, ask: Is it % or number? Is it exact or approximate (use "just under 50%", "around 42%")? Did I use correct tense (past for past years, present for maps with no time)? Did I compare, not just report?

    The line graph below shows the number of overseas tourists, in millions, who visited France, Spain, and Japan between 1990 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

    Overseas Tourist Arrivals, 1990–2020
    France, Spain, and Japan (millions)
    France
    Spain
    Japan

    ⭐ Band 9 Model Answer

    Click "Show on chart" to see exactly which line(s)/point(s) each sentence describes.

    Introduction The line graph illustrates the number of overseas tourists, measured in millions, who visited France, Spain, and Japan between 1990 and 2020.
    Overview Overall, France and Spain both experienced substantial growth in tourist numbers for the majority of the period before declining sharply towards the end, whereas Japan's figures remained comparatively low and relatively stable throughout, only rising modestly before falling back close to its starting level.
    ✦ Overview Technique
    Two Patterns, Zero Numbers
    This Overview identifies the "rise then sharp fall" pattern shared by France and Spain, and contrasts it with Japan's "low and stable" pattern — exactly the analytical grouping a Band 9 Overview requires, without quoting a single figure.
    Body Paragraph 1 — France and Spain France recorded the highest number of visitors throughout almost the entire period, rising from 52 million in 1990 to a peak of 77 million in 2010, before falling steeply to just 40 million by 2020. Spain followed a broadly similar trajectory, climbing from 34 million to a high of 53 million in 2010, then dropping to 19 million in the final year shown — its lowest figure across the whole thirty-year period.
    ✦ Peak + Fall Structure
    Three-Point Anchoring (start, peak, end)
    Rather than just start/end, this sentence anchors THREE points — the 1990 start, the 2010 peak, and the 2020 end — which is necessary here because the line doesn't move in one direction only. This is more precise than the simple two-point method used for monotonic lines.
    ✦ Comparison + Superlative
    "Broadly Similar Trajectory" + "Lowest Figure"
    Explicitly comparing Spain's shape to France's avoids treating the two lines as unrelated. "Its lowest figure across the whole... period" is a superlative claim verified directly from the chart — precise, not a rounded guess.
    Body Paragraph 2 — Japan By contrast, Japan attracted far fewer tourists throughout, with figures never exceeding single digits until reaching a modest peak of 9 million in 2010. Interestingly, despite the dramatic declines seen in France and Spain, Japan's 2020 figure of 4 million represented a comparatively smaller fall relative to its own earlier peak.
    ✦ "By Contrast" Signals New Group
    Clear Paragraph-Level Contrast
    Opening Body ¶2 with "By contrast" immediately signals this line behaves differently from the pair just discussed — giving the response clear structural signposting.
    ✦ Cross-Line Interpretive Comparison
    Relative Scale Comparison Across All Three Lines
    This sentence performs a genuinely analytical comparison — Japan's fall is "smaller" not in absolute terms but "relative to its own earlier peak," implicitly contrasting with France and Spain's much larger absolute and proportional declines. This kind of cross-line synthesis is rare and impressive at Band 9.

    The 4-Paragraph Bar Chart Formula

    Introduction (~25 words)

    • Paraphrase what is being compared
    • State the unit of measurement

    Overview (~30 words)

    • Identify the highest and lowest categories
    • Note any obvious clusters/tiers — no numbers yet

    🏫 Reported May 2026

    The chart below shows the percentage of male and female graduates in six different subjects at a UK university in 2025. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

    Graduates by Subject and Gender, 2025

    ⭐ Band 9 Model Answer

    Click "Show on chart" to see exactly which bar(s) each sentence describes.

    Introduction The bar chart compares the percentage of male and female students who graduated in six different subjects at a UK university in 2025.
    Overview Overall, clear gender imbalances were evident in several subjects, with men considerably outnumbering women in the STEM-related fields of Engineering and Computer Science, while the reverse pattern was observed in Nursing and Psychology; only Business and Law showed a relatively even split between the sexes.
    ✦ Overview Technique
    Three-Way Grouping in One Sentence
    This Overview identifies THREE distinct patterns — male-skewed, female-skewed, and balanced subjects — in a single, efficiently structured sentence, without citing any specific percentage.
    Body Paragraph 1 — Subjects with a strong gender skew The most pronounced gender disparities were found in Engineering and Computer Science, where male graduates accounted for 78% and 82% of the respective cohorts, leaving female graduates considerably underrepresented at just 22% and 18%. The pattern was reversed in Nursing and Psychology, subjects in which women made up the clear majority of graduates, at 88% and 75% respectively, while men comprised only 12% and 25%.
    ✦ Paired Figures in One Sentence
    "78% and 82%... 22% and 18%" — Efficient Dual Reporting
    Reporting two subjects' figures together in one sentence, using "respective cohorts" to keep the pairing clear, is more efficient than four separate sentences — while "considerably underrepresented" adds evaluative precision.
    ✦ Explicit Cross-Reference
    "Pattern Was Reversed"
    Explicitly stating the pattern is "reversed" ties this pair of subjects back to the previous sentence's pattern — demonstrating cohesion across the whole paragraph rather than four disconnected facts.
    Body Paragraph 2 — Balanced subjects By contrast, Business and Law displayed a far more balanced gender distribution. In Business, the split was almost even, with 54% of graduates being male and 46% female. Law showed a similarly narrow gap, although here women were marginally in the majority, accounting for 55% of graduates compared to 45% of men.

    🎯 Selection

    The 80/20 Rule for Process - Natural

    In Task 1 you have 20 minutes. You cannot describe everything. Examiners reward overview + key features, not data dumping. For Process - Natural, focus on cyclical stages, active/passive mix, natural processes.

    📊
    Selection Formula for Process - Natural
    Step 1: Identify overall trend/pattern in 10 seconds (up? down? stable? transformation?).
    Step 2: Pick 2-3 most significant data points (highest, lowest, most dramatic change).
    Step 3: Group similar data (e.g., "All European countries declined except...").
    Step 4: Ignore minor fluctuations under 5% unless they show a pattern.
    Step 5: Always include a comparison (not just "X was 50%" but "X was double Y").

    ✅ Include

    • Overall trend/change
    • Highest/lowest values with figures
    • Most dramatic increase/decrease
    • Comparisons (twice as much, similar to)
    • Exceptions to the trend

    ❌ Ignore

    • Every single data point
    • Minor 1-2% fluctuations
    • Repeating same structure 5 times
    • Personal opinion ("I think this is good")
    • Data you don't understand — skip smartly
    ⚡ Process - Natural Specific Trick: For Process - Natural, use the "3+1" method: 3 main features + 1 exception/interesting detail.     👁 Overview

    Why 60% Fail Task Achievement on Process - Natural

    The overview is NOT an introduction. It's a summary of main trends/features, placed after intro, with NO figures. Many students write "The chart shows..." and then jump to details — that's not an overview.

    🏗
    The Universal Overview Formula for Process - Natural
    Sentence 1: Overall trend/pattern (no numbers). "Overall, [X] experienced a significant [increase/decline/transformation] over the period, while [Y] remained relatively stable."
    Sentence 2: Most notable detail/comparison (still no numbers, or only general). "In particular, [category] accounted for the largest share throughout, whereas [other] saw the most dramatic change."
    For Maps: "Overall, the area underwent extensive development, transforming from a predominantly rural to a largely urban landscape, with the most notable changes occurring in the north-east."
    For Process: "Overall, the process comprises [number] main stages, beginning with [first] and culminating in [last], and is largely [linear/cyclical/mechanical]."

    Introduction The diagram illustrates the life cycle of a honeybee, from the laying of eggs through to the production of honey within the hive.
    Overview Overall, the cycle consists of five interconnected stages and operates continuously, with each new generation of bees ultimately contributing to the honey production that, in turn, sustains the colony and enables further reproduction.
    ✦ Overview Technique
    Continuity Stated + the Loop Explained
    Beyond simply naming five stages, this Overview explains HOW the cycle closes — honey sustains the colony, which enables further reproduction — previewing the causal loop that Body ¶2 will describe in detail.
    Body Paragraph 1 — Egg, larva, and pupa Beginning with the egg stage for the purposes of this description, the queen bee lays a single egg in each individual cell of the honeycomb. After approximately three days, the egg hatches into a larva, which is fed continuously by worker bees until it grows large enough to fill its cell. The larva then enters the pupa stage, during which it is sealed within its cell and undergoes complete metamorphosis, developing the physical features of an adult bee.
    ✦ Band 9 Technique
    Acknowledging the Arbitrary Starting Point
    "For the purposes of this description" signals that the writer understands this cycle has no true beginning — egg is simply a convenient, commonly-used entry point.
    ✦ Band 9 Technique
    Specific Time Reference
    "After approximately three days" gives the process concrete pacing — far more precise than a vague "after some time," while "approximately" correctly hedges since exact durations vary.
    ✦ Band 9 Technique
    Technical Term Used Precisely
    "Undergoes complete metamorphosis" is the correct biological term for this transformation, used accurately in context rather than a vague paraphrase like "changes into a bee."
    Body Paragraph 2 — Adult bee, honey production, and the cycle restarting Once metamorphosis is complete, the adult bee emerges from its cell and, after a short period of internal hive duties, begins foraging for nectar from flowers. This nectar is carried back to the hive, where it is processed by worker bees and stored in honeycomb cells as honey, providing food for the colony, including the next generation of larvae. At this point, the cycle begins again as the queen continues laying new eggs, sustained by the honey the colony has produced.
    ✦ Band 9 Technique
    "Once + Adjective" Clause Reduction
    "Once metamorphosis is complete" is an economical reduced clause (rather than "once the metamorphosis has been completed") — grammatically efficient and natural-sounding.
    ✦ Band 9 Technique
    Explicit Cycle Restart, Closing the Loop
    "The cycle begins again... sustained by the honey the colony has produced" explicitly confirms the causal loop previewed in the Overview — honey enables the queen to keep laying eggs, closing the cycle in a concrete, specific way rather than a generic "and it repeats."
